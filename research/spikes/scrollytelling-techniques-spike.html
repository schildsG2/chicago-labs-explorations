Scrollytelling Techniques — Delighters Without Video or 3D

Sep 18, 2026

1. Executive Summary

Sourced from a 7-technique breakdown of visual scrollytelling (video transcript, §2) plus a direct audit of four live scrollytelling pieces (§3): three agency/portfolio sites — mariajoaoabrantes.work, illoca.unseen.co, and goats.com.pl — plus one recent, widely-shared editorial piece added specifically for its public reach: The Pudding's "Growing Up With K-Pop" (May 2026).

Our constraint: no video, no 3D assets. The content we're actually telling a story with — wizard answers and product data (see the content handoff) — is text and structured data, not footage. Two of the three example sites (illoca, goats) lean heavily on custom photography/illustration we don't have and won't fabricate. That rules out visual metaphor via photography, but doesn't rule out the underlying techniques.

Recommendation: lean hardest on three of the seven techniques — typography as a visual element, purposeful scroll-driven animation (progressive reveal, not scroll-jacking), and data visualization as narrative (a match/confidence meter, not a raw number) — backed by strict contrast/hierarchy and white space discipline. Visual metaphor is still achievable, just through icon/diagram/card composition instead of photography or footage. See §4 for the concrete toolkit.

2. The 7 techniques (source: video transcript)

A YouTube breakdown (youtube.com/watch?v=U0COL050HlU) names seven techniques that separate content that uses visuals from content that merely has them. Distilled here with an honest read on whether each is usable given our no-video/no-3D constraint.

Technique What it means Usable here?
Visual flow Where the eye goes is a design choice — alignment, white space, and animation should tell the reader "now this," not leave them to find the entry point themselves. Yes — fully achievable with layout, spacing, and scroll order. No special assets needed.
Contrast & hierarchy If everything is bold, nothing is. Differences in size/color/weight create a reading path; a page with no contrast has nowhere for the eye to land. Yes — this is a discipline, not an asset. Directly enforced by Elevate's type scale and semantic color tokens.
White space Breathing room, not empty space. Prevents the "too much competing for attention" reaction that makes a reader bail. Yes — same as above, a layout discipline.
Purposeful animation Every animation needs a function — guide the eye, reveal information progressively, or reinforce the message. Otherwise, cut it. Yes — scroll-triggered progressive reveal (fade/slide-in tied to scroll position) needs no video or 3D, just CSS/JS tied to scroll or IntersectionObserver.
Visual metaphor Show, don't tell — a plant sprouting for "growth," interlocking pieces for "connection." The image carries the concept faster than the stat does. Partial. No photography or 3D to stage a metaphor in. Achievable instead through iconography, simple diagrams, or card composition (see §4).
Typography as a visual element Type isn't just a readability decision — variable weight, strategic sizing, and progressive reveal on scroll make text something the reader experiences, not just reads. Yes — our strongest lever. The content is fundamentally text (wizard answers, product copy), so this is where most of the "delight" budget should go.
Data visualization Raw numbers have no inherent shape — a chart should answer a question, not just display information. Reveal progressively rather than dumping a full chart at once. Yes — central to this epic. Ratings, pricing, and (eventually) a match/confidence value are exactly the kind of numbers that need a shape.

3. Site audit — what each example actually does

Notes from directly scrolling each site, not secondhand description. These are creative/portfolio sites — treat as a pattern library to borrow mechanics from, not a visual style to copy wholesale.

Also see the standalone "recent + trending" entry below (3.4) — a live editorial piece rather than an agency portfolio, included because it's the kind of thing that's actually in public consciousness right now, not just design-award-circle inspiration.

mariajoaoabrantes.work

  • Oversized display type is the primary visual on the hero — no photo or video, the headline alone carries the page.
  • Sentence-style case-study headline with one clause set in the brand's lavender accent color, carrying the outcome metric inline: "...— cutting participant sourcing effort by 84%." The stat is embedded in a sentence, not boxed in a separate stat card.
  • Uppercase, em-dash-separated tag rows under each case study (e.g. "RESEARCH OPS — PRODUCT DESIGN — ACCESSIBILITY") as lightweight metadata/context chips.
  • Horizontally scrollable category filter bar near the top for jumping between case studies.
  • Device-mockup frames (browser chrome, phone frames) contain every UI screenshot — instantly reads as "real product," not a floating image.
  • A persistent floating side badge and a sticky bottom-corner nav pill provide wayfinding without a fixed header eating vertical space.

illoca.unseen.co

  • A sketchbook/graph-paper background texture persists across the whole page — reinforces the "architectural drafting" metaphor at the chrome level, not just in imagery.
  • Hand-drawn marker annotations (underlines, arrows, a circled numeral) are overlaid directly on set display type — turns plain typography into an annotated diagram.
  • A duotone illustration crossfades between two rendering states (a photorealistic-feeling scene → a line-sketch of the same scene) as the reader scrolls — a scroll-scrubbed transform used to narrate a before/after or process step without any video.
  • Numbered step badges in a hand-drawn circle ("①") pair with each section headline, making a multi-step narrative sequence explicit.
  • The nav bar stays pinned through full-bleed image sections rather than disappearing.

goats.com.pl

  • The hero headline reveals word-by-word/clause-by-clause via opacity tied directly to scroll position, with the first clause locked in brand pink while the rest is still fading in — a pure typographic scroll effect, no imagery required.
  • A section headline ("WHAT WE DO") is staged literally inside a stock photo — a person holding up a newspaper whose front page is the headline. A visual metaphor achieved through photo composition + text, not motion or 3D.
  • Case studies sit in a horizontal scroll-snap card carousel nested inside the page's normal vertical scroll.
  • Full-bleed color/tone inversion between sections (light → near-black) works as a scene-break device, signaling a shift in topic without a visible divider.
  • A floating circular menu button and a fixed social-icon rail provide persistent chrome that doesn't compete with content.
Recent · trending, not just design-circle inspiration

3.4 The Pudding — "Growing Up With K-Pop"

Published May 2026. Two real friends narrate their friendship across five K-pop "generations."

Aesthetic note — off-brand, don't copy the look: the visual language here (glossy chrome/bubble-letter chapter titles, shifting pastel sunset gradients, cutesy illustrated avatars, hand-drawn-sticker styling) is firmly outside Elevate and outside what fits our product. This entry is here for the mechanics below, not the visual style — none of it should inform color, type treatment, or illustration choices in our explorations.
  • Sticky chapter-nav doubling as a progress tracker — a persistent top bar with one icon per "generation" (chapter), the active one highlighted, so the reader always knows where they are in a multi-part narrative. Directly reusable as a lightweight, Elevate-styled step indicator instead of hand-drawn icons.
  • Attributed quote/speech-bubble cards — each narrator gets an avatar + name tag + a card holding her side of the story, letting two "voices" interleave without needing photos or video of real people. Maps cleanly onto rendering a buyer's own wizard answers as an attributed card ("you told us...") distinct from the system's response.
  • Stylized media-reference card standing in for real video — a "sticker"-style card (thumbnail + play icon + title) references a song/video without actually embedding playable media. This is the direct workaround for our no-video constraint: a card that represents a rich-media reference (e.g. a product screenshot or demo) rather than the real thing.

4. Toolkit for this epic — typography, data-vis, cards

Mapping the achievable techniques onto the actual narrative this epic has to tell: a buyer's wizard answers → how they were weighted → a recommended product (see the content handoff for the real data fields and per-product attributes available).

Technique Where it fits
Personalization sentence w/ inline highlighted keyword Replace the flat "Based on your criteria: chip · chip · chip" pattern (handoff §5) with a sentence that highlights one clause, mirroring mariajoaoabrantes.work — e.g. "Because you need things to work together, we weighted integration support heavily."
Scroll-scrubbed progressive reveal Reveal each narrative beat (what you told us → how we weighed it → your top match) one at a time as the reader scrolls, instead of rendering the full page at once. IntersectionObserver-driven fade/slide, not scroll-jacking.
Numbered step badges Borrow illoca's hand-drawn-circle numeral pattern (rendered in Elevate style, not literally hand-drawn) to make the 3-beat sequence explicit and scannable.
Data-vis as narrative, not dashboard Render a per-product match/confidence value as a single meter, arc, or ring tied to a plain-language callout ("strong match because...") rather than a raw percentage in a table. Since no scoring engine exists yet (handoff §3), design the visualization to read correctly with a placeholder value.
Card-based product reveal With no video/3D, product identity/rating/pricing/pros-cons render as Elevate cards (Elevate::ProductDetails::Component, Elevate::StarRating::Component — handoff §4) revealed one at a time via scroll, rather than stacked all at once like the superseded compare-page layout.
Section tone inversion A background shift (neutral → purple-tinted, or light → dark) between "your answers" and "your recommendation" sections signals the pivot from input to payoff, echoing goats.com.pl's light/dark scene breaks.
Sticky/pinned summary rail A persistent sidebar summarizing "what we know about you so far" that fills in as the reader scrolls — the same sticky-visual mechanic all three sites use, just built from text/cards instead of an image.
Sticky chapter-nav / progress tracker Borrow the mechanic (not the look) from §3.4's "Growing Up With K-Pop" — a persistent top bar with one icon per narrative beat, active one highlighted, rendered in plain Elevate style (no bubble letters, no illustrated icons).
Attributed quote card Render each wizard answer back to the buyer as an attributed card ("you told us...") distinct from the system's response card, echoing §3.4's two-narrator speech-bubble pattern — using Elevate card styling, not avatars/illustration.
Stylized media-reference card Where the handoff (§4) mentions screenshots or a demo, use a "sticker"-style reference card (thumbnail + label) rather than an embedded player — the same workaround §3.4 uses to reference a song without embedding real video.

5. Explicitly out of scope / open questions

  • No video, no 3D-rendered assets, no custom photography-based visual metaphors — both illoca and goats lean on custom photography/illustration budgets we don't have and shouldn't fabricate.
  • No scroll-jacking — hijacked scroll speed/physics contradicts the video's core rule ("every animation needs a function") and Elevate's motion-should-be-subtle stance. Standard scroll-triggered reveal only.
  • Open: is there any icon/illustration budget for visual metaphor, or is typography + data-vis + cards the entire toolkit for this epic?
  • Open: confidence/match scoring doesn't exist yet (handoff §3) — explorations should design the data-vis to work with a placeholder value, and flag "how confidence is computed" back as a dependency rather than solving it here.
